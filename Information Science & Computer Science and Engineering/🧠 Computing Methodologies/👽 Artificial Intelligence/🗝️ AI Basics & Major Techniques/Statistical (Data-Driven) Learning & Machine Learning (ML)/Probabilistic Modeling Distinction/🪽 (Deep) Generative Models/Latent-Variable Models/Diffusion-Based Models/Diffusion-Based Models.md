## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion_model

In [machine learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_learning "Machine learning"), **diffusion models**, also known as **diffusion-based generative models** or **score-based generative models**, are a class of [latent variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latent_variable_model "Latent variable model") [generative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_model "Generative model") models. A diffusion model consists of two major components: the forward diffusion process, and the reverse sampling process. The goal of diffusion models is to learn a [diffusion process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion_process "Diffusion process") for a given dataset, such that the process can generate new elements that are distributed similarly as the original dataset. A diffusion model models data as generated by a diffusion process, whereby a new datum performs a [random walk with drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener_process "Wiener process") through the space of all possible data. A trained diffusion model can be sampled in many ways, with different efficiency and quality.

There are various equivalent formalisms, including [Markov chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_chain "Markov chain"), denoising diffusion probabilistic models, noise conditioned score networks, and stochastic differential equations. They are typically trained using [variational inference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variational_Bayesian_methods "Variational Bayesian methods"). The model responsible for denoising is typically called its "[backbone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion_model#Choice_of_architecture)". The backbone may be of any kind, but they are typically [U-nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-Net "U-Net") or [transformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformer_\(deep_learning_architecture\) "Transformer (deep learning architecture)").

As of 2024, diffusion models are mainly used for [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision "Computer vision") tasks, including [image denoising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_denoising "Image denoising"), [inpainting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inpainting "Inpainting"), [super-resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super-resolution "Super-resolution"), [image generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text-to-image_model "Text-to-image model"), and video generation. These typically involve training a neural network to sequentially [denoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denoise "Denoise") images blurred with [Gaussian noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_noise "Gaussian noise"). The model is trained to reverse the process of adding noise to an image. After training to convergence, it can be used for image generation by starting with an image composed of random noise, and applying the network iteratively to denoise the image.

Diffusion-based image generators have seen widespread commercial interest, such as [Stable Diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_Diffusion "Stable Diffusion") and [DALL-E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DALL-E "DALL-E"). These models typically combine diffusion models with other models, such as text-encoders and cross-attention modules to allow text-conditioned generation.

Other than computer vision, diffusion models have also found applications in [natural language processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_language_processing "Natural language processing") such as [text generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_language_generation "Natural language generation") and [summarization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_summarization "Automatic summarization"), sound generation, and [reinforcement learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinforcement_learning "Reinforcement learning").
