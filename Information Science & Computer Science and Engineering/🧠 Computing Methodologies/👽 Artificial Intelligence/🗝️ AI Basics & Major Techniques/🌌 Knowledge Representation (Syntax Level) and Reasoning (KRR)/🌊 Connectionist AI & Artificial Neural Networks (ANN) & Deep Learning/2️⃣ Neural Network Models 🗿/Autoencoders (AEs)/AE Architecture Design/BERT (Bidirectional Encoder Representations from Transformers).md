## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BERT_(language_model)

Bidirectional encoder representations from transformers (BERT) is a language model introduced in October 2018 by researchers at Google.[2][3] It learns to represent text as a sequence of vectors using self-supervised learning. It uses the encoder-only transformer architecture. BERT dramatically improved the state of the art for large language models. As of 2026, BERT is a common methodological component in natural language processing (NLP) research.[4][5]

BERT is trained by masked token prediction and next sentence prediction. With this training, BERT learns contextual, latent representations of tokens in their context, similar to ELMo and GPT-2.[6] It found applications for many natural language processing tasks, such as coreference resolution and polysemy resolution.[7] It improved on ELMo and spawned the study of "BERTology", which attempts to interpret what is learned by BERT.[4]

BERT was originally implemented in the English language at two model sizes, BERTBASE (110 million parameters) and BERTLARGE (340 million parameters). Both were trained on the Toronto BookCorpus[8] (800M words) and English Wikipedia (2,500M words).[2]: 5  The weights were released on GitHub.[9] On March 11, 2020, 24 smaller models were released, the smallest being BERTTINY with just 4 million parameters.[9]
