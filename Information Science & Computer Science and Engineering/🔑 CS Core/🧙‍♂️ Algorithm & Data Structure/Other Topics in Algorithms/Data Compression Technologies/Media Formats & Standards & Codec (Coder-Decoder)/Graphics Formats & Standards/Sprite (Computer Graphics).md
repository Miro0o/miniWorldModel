## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprite_(computer_graphics)

In [computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics "Computer graphics"), a **sprite** is a [two-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_\(mathematics\) "Plane (mathematics)") [bitmap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitmap "Bitmap") that is integrated into a larger scene, most often in a 2D [video game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game "Video game"). Originally, the term _sprite_ referred to fixed-sized objects composited together, by hardware, with a background. Use of the term has since become more general.

Systems with hardware sprites include [arcade video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_video_game "Arcade video game") of the 1970s and 1980s; [game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_consoles "Game consoles") including as the [Atari VCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_VCS "Atari VCS") (1977), [ColecoVision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ColecoVision "ColecoVision") (1982), [Famicom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_Entertainment_System "Nintendo Entertainment System") (1983), [Genesis/Mega Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega_Genesis "Sega Genesis") (1988); and [home computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_computers "Home computers") such as the [TI-99/4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TI-99/4 "TI-99/4") (1979), [Atari 8-bit computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_8-bit_computers "Atari 8-bit computers") (1979), [Commodore 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_64 "Commodore 64") (1982), [MSX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSX "MSX") (1983), [Amiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amiga "Amiga") (1985), and [X68000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X68000 "X68000") (1987). Hardware varies in the number of sprites supported, the size and colors of each sprite, and special effects such as scaling or reporting pixel-precise overlap.

Hardware composition of sprites occurs as each [scan line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scan_line "Scan line") is prepared for the video output device, such as a [cathode-ray tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathode-ray_tube "Cathode-ray tube"), without involvement of the main [CPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_processing_unit "Central processing unit") and without the need for a full-screen [frame buffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_buffer "Frame buffer"). Sprites can be positioned or altered by setting attributes used during the hardware composition process. The number of sprites which can be displayed per scan line is often lower than the total number of sprites a system supports. For example, the Texas Instruments [TMS9918](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TMS9918 "TMS9918") chip supports 32 sprites, but only four can appear on the same scan line.

The CPUs in modern computers, video game consoles, and mobile devices are fast enough that bitmaps can be drawn into a frame buffer without special hardware assistance. Beyond that, [GPUs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPU "GPU") can render vast numbers of scaled, rotated, [anti-aliased](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-aliased "Anti-aliased"), partially translucent, very high resolution images in parallel with the CPU.
