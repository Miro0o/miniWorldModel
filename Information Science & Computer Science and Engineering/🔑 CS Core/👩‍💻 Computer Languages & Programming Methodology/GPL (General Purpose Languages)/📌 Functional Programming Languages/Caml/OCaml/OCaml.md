## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OCaml

**OCaml** ([/oʊˈkæməl/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [_oh-KAM-əl_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"), formerly **Objective Caml**) is a [general-purpose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General-purpose_programming_language "General-purpose programming language"), [high-level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-level_programming_language "High-level programming language"), [multi-paradigm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparison_of_multi-paradigm_programming_languages "Comparison of multi-paradigm programming languages") [programming language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programming_language "Programming language") which extends the [Caml](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caml "Caml") dialect of [ML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ML_\(programming_language\) "ML (programming language)") with [object-oriented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object-oriented_programming "Object-oriented programming") features. OCaml was created in 1996 by [Xavier Leroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xavier_Leroy "Xavier Leroy"), Jérôme Vouillon, [Damien Doligez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damien_Doligez "Damien Doligez"), Didier Rémy, Ascánder Suárez, and others.

The OCaml [toolchain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toolchain "Toolchain") includes an interactive top-level [interpreter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpreter_\(computing\) "Interpreter (computing)"), a [bytecode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bytecode "Bytecode") [compiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compiler "Compiler"), an optimizing [machine code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_code "Machine code") compiler, a reversible [debugger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debugger "Debugger"), and a [package manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Package_manager "Package manager") ([OPAM](https://opam.ocaml.org/packages/)) together with a composable build system for OCaml ([Dune](https://dune.build/)). OCaml was developed first in the context of [automated theorem proving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_theorem_proving "Automated theorem proving"), and is used in [static analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Static_program_analysis "Static program analysis") and [formal methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_methods "Formal methods") software. Beyond these areas, it has found use in [systems programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_programming "Systems programming"), [web development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_development "Web development"), and specific financial utilities, among other application domains.

The acronym _CAML_ originally stood for _Categorical Abstract Machine Language_, but OCaml omits this [abstract machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_machine "Abstract machine"). OCaml is a [free and open-source software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_open-source_software "Free and open-source software") project managed and principally maintained by the [French Institute for Research in Computer Science and Automation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Institute_for_Research_in_Computer_Science_and_Automation "French Institute for Research in Computer Science and Automation") (Inria). In the early 2000s, elements from OCaml were adopted by many languages, notably [F#](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F_Sharp_\(programming_language\) "F Sharp (programming language)") and [Scala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scala_\(programming_language\) "Scala (programming language)").
