## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_model

A **statistical model** is a [mathematical model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_model "Mathematical model") that embodies a set of [statistical assumptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_assumptions "Statistical assumptions") concerning the generation of [sample data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_\(statistics\) "Sample (statistics)") (and similar data from a larger [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_population "Statistical population")). A statistical model represents, often in considerably idealized form, the [data-generating process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_generating_process "Data generating process"). When referring specifically to [probabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability "Probability"), the corresponding term is **probabilistic model**. All [statistical hypothesis tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_hypothesis_testing "Statistical hypothesis testing") and all [statistical estimators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estimator "Estimator") are derived via statistical models. More generally, statistical models are part of the foundation of [statistical inference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_inference "Statistical inference"). A statistical model is usually specified as a mathematical relationship between one or more [random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_variables "Random variables") and other non-random variables. As such, a statistical model is "a formal representation of a theory" ([Herman Adèr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_J._Ad%C3%A8r "Herman J. Adèr") quoting [Kenneth Bollen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_A._Bollen "Kenneth A. Bollen")).
