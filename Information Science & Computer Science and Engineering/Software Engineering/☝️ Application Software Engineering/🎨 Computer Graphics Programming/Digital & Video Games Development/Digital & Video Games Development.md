## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game

A **video game**, **computer game**, or simply **game** is an [electronic game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_game "Electronic game") that involves interaction with a [user interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/User_interface "User interface") or [input device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Input_device "Input device") (such as a [joystick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joystick "Joystick"), [controller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_controller "Game controller"), [keyboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_keyboard "Computer keyboard"), or [motion sensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_sensing "Motion sensing") device) to generate [visual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual "Visual") [feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feedback "Feedback") from a [display device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Display_device "Display device"), most commonly shown in a [video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video "Video") format on a [television set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_set "Television set"), [computer monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_monitor "Computer monitor"), [flat-panel display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat-panel_display "Flat-panel display") or [touchscreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touchscreen "Touchscreen") on [handheld devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handheld_device "Handheld device"), or a [virtual reality headset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_reality_headset "Virtual reality headset"). Most modern video games are [audiovisual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audiovisual "Audiovisual"), with [audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound "Sound") complement delivered through [speakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loudspeaker "Loudspeaker") or [headphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headphones "Headphones"), and sometimes also with other types of sensory feedback (e.g., [haptic technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haptic_technology "Haptic technology") that provides [tactile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touch "Touch") sensations). Some video games also allow [microphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microphone "Microphone") and [webcam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webcam "Webcam") inputs for [in-game chatting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_chat_in_online_gaming "Voice chat in online gaming") and [livestreaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_livestreaming "Video game livestreaming").

Video games are typically categorized according to their [hardware platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardware_platform "Hardware platform"), which traditionally includes [arcade video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_video_game "Arcade video game"), [console games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Console_game "Console game"), and [computer games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PC_game "PC game") (which includes [LAN games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_game "Network game"), [online games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_game "Online game"), and [browser games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Browser_game "Browser game")). More recently, the [video game industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_industry "Video game industry") has expanded onto [mobile gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_gaming "Mobile gaming") through [mobile devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_device "Mobile device") (such as [smartphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone "Smartphone") and [tablet computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tablet_computer "Tablet computer")), [virtual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_reality "Virtual reality") and [augmented reality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmented_reality "Augmented reality") systems, and remote [cloud gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_gaming "Cloud gaming"). Video games are also classified into a wide range of [genres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_genre "Video game genre") based on their style of [gameplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gameplay "Gameplay") and [target audience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_audience "Target audience").

The first video game prototypes in the 1950s and 1960s were simple extensions of electronic games using video-like output from large, room-sized [mainframe computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainframe_computer "Mainframe computer"). The first consumer video game was the arcade video game _[Computer Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Space "Computer Space")_ in 1971, which took inspiration from the earlier 1962 computer game _[Spacewar!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacewar! "Spacewar!")_. In 1972 came the now-iconic video game _[Pong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pong "Pong")_ and the first [home console](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_video_game_console "Home video game console"), the [Magnavox Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnavox_Odyssey "Magnavox Odyssey"). The industry grew quickly during the ["golden age" of arcade video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_age_of_arcade_video_games "Golden age of arcade video games") from the late 1970s to early 1980s, and companies like [Atari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari "Atari"), [Mattel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattel "Mattel"), and [Coleco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coleco "Coleco") also released highly successful [consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_console "Video game console") for the home market: however, loss of publishing control and saturation of the console gaming market led to the [crash of the North American video game market in 1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_video_game_crash "1983 video game crash"). Following the crash, the industry matured and diversified: by the late 1980s video game consoles became dominated by Japanese companies such as [Nintendo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo "Nintendo"), [Sega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega "Sega"), and later [Sony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Interactive_Entertainment "Sony Interactive Entertainment"), whereas the [Microsoft Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows "Microsoft Windows") (and [MS-DOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS-DOS "MS-DOS") before it) line of operating systems increasingly came to dominate the computer gaming space, with multiplayer online PC games seeing the establishment of the nascent [eSports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESports "ESports") scene. Arcades on the other hand steadily lost their technological edge and popularity by the end of the 1990s in the face of advancements in the console and computer space, especially in North America and Europe, while in the [handheld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handheld_game_console "Handheld game console") space Nintendo achieved dominance with the [Game Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Boy "Game Boy") line. By the 2000s, Sega exited the console space, and from then on the landscape has been dominated by the "big three" console manufacturers of Nintendo, Sony, and [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft"). At this time the core industry also increasingly centered on "[AAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AAA_\(video_game_industry\) "AAA (video game industry)")" games, leaving less room for riskier, experimental, and lower-budget games. Coupled with increasing availability of high-speed [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet "Internet") and [digital distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_distribution_of_video_games "Digital distribution of video games"), this gave room for [independent video game development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_video_game_development "Independent video game development") (or "[indie games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_game "Indie game")") to gain prominence into the 2010s. Since then, the commercial importance of the [video game industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_industry "Video game industry") has been increasing. The emerging Asian markets and proliferation of smartphone games in particular are altering player demographics towards [casual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casual_games "Casual games") and [cozy gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cozy_game "Cozy game"), and increasing [monetization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_monetization "Video game monetization") by incorporating [games as a service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Games_as_a_service "Games as a service").

Today, [video game development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_development "Video game development") requires numerous skills, [vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foresight_\(psychology\) "Foresight (psychology)"), [teamwork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teamwork "Teamwork"), and [liaisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collaboration "Collaboration") between different parties, including [developers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_developers "Video game developers"), [publishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_publishers "Video game publishers"), [distributors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_\(marketing\) "Distribution (marketing)"), [retailers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retailer "Retailer"), [hardware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_hardware "Electronic hardware") manufacturers, and other marketers, to successfully bring a game to its consumers. As of 2020, the global video game market had estimated annual revenues of US$159 billion across hardware, software, and services, which is three times the size of the global [music industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_industry "Music industry") and four times that of the [film industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_industry "Film industry") in 2019, making it a formidable heavyweight across the modern [entertainment industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_industry "Entertainment industry"). The video game market is also a major influence behind the [electronics industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics_industry "Electronics industry"), where [personal computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_computer "Personal computer") component, console, and peripheral sales, as well as consumer demands for better game performance, have been powerful driving factors for hardware design and innovation.

### Digital Games Genres
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_genre

A video game genre is an informal classification of a video game based on how it is played rather than visual or narrative elements.[1][2] This is independent of setting, unlike works of fiction that are expressed through other media, such as films or books. For example, a shooter game is still a shooter game, or a puzzle game is still a puzzle game, regardless of where or when it takes place.[3][4] A specific game's genre is open to subjective interpretation. An individual game may belong to several genres at once.[1]

> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_video_game_genres

A video game genre is a specific category of games related by similar gameplay characteristics. Video game genres are not usually defined by the setting or story of the game or its medium of play, but by the way the player interacts with the game.[1] For example, a first-person shooter is still a first-person shooter regardless of whether it takes place in a science fiction, western, fantasy, or military setting, so long as it features a camera mimicking the perspective of the protagonist (first-person) and gameplay centered around the use of ranged weaponry.

Genres may encompass a wide variety of games, leading to even more specific classifications called subgenres. For example, an action game can be classified into many subgenres such as platform games and fighting games. Some games, most notably browser and mobile games, are commonly classified into multiple genres.[1][2]
