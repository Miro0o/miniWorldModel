## Intro
### Zaibatsu & Keiretsu (財閥／ざいばつ)
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaibatsu

_**Zaibatsu**_ (財閥; lit. '[asset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset "Asset") [clique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clique "Clique")') is a [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language") term referring to industrial and financial [vertically integrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_integration "Vertical integration") business [conglomerates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conglomerate_\(company\) "Conglomerate (company)") in the [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan"), whose influence and size allowed control over significant parts of the [Japanese economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Japan "Economy of Japan") from the [Meiji era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_era "Meiji era") to [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). A zaibatsu's general structure included a family-owned holding company on top, and a bank which financed the other, mostly industrial subsidiaries within them. Although the zaibatsu played an important role in the Japanese economy beginning in 1868, they especially increased in number and importance following the [Russo-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Japanese_War "Russo-Japanese War"), [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), and Japan's subsequent attempt to conquer [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia") and the [Pacific Rim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Rim "Pacific Rim") during the [interwar period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period "Interwar period") and World War II. After World War II, they were dissolved by the [Allied occupation forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Japan "Occupation of Japan") and succeeded by the _[keiretsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keiretsu "Keiretsu")_ (groups of banks, manufacturers, suppliers, and distributors). Equivalents to the zaibatsu can still be found in other countries, such as the _[chaebol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaebol "Chaebol")_ conglomerates of [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea").

> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keiretsu

A _**keiretsu**_ ([Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language"): [系列](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E8%AA%9E%E3%81%8B%E3%82%89%E8%8B%B1%E8%AA%9E%E3%81%B8%E3%81%AE%E5%80%9F%E7%94%A8#J.2C_K.2C_L "ja:日本語から英語への借用"); literally _system_, _series_, _grouping of enterprises_, _order of succession_) is a set of [companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company "Company") with interlocking business relationships and [shareholdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shareholder "Shareholder") that dominated the [Japanese economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Japan "Economy of Japan") in the second half of the 20th century. In the legal sense, it is a type of [business group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_group "Business group") that is in a loosely organized alliance within Japan's business community. It rose up to replace the _[zaibatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaibatsu "Zaibatsu")_ system that was dissolved in the [occupation of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Japan "Occupation of Japan") following the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War"). Though their influence has shrunk since the late 20th century, they continue to be important forces in Japan's economy in the early 21st century.

The members' companies own small portions of the shares in each other's companies, centered on a core bank; this system helps insulate each company from stock market fluctuations and [takeover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeover "Takeover") attempts, thus enabling long-term planning in projects.
